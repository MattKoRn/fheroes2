# Homm2RPG

The [24 September 2026 update](RPG_UPDATE_2026-09-24.md) strengthens offline snapshot recovery, prevents arithmetic overflow in long offline sessions, preserves useful RPG profile backups, and fixes Windows compilation of RPG-aware hero AI.

Homm2RPG adds a persistent, kingdom-wide RPG profile to fheroes2. Press **F9** on the adventure map to open the menu, or **press and hold the Kingdom button** for touch/controller-friendly access; a normal tap still opens Kingdom Summary. The RPG screen uses a Heroes II-style royal-guild presentation: gem-decorated window framing, embossed/inset brown-and-gold panels, pressed category plaques, framed rank crests, guild-themed section names, and compact BUY plaques. Every visible upgrade row shows its current combat effect and the exact next-rank effect before you spend a point; unaffordable BUY plaques show the missing guild-point amount directly. Right-click unit information now shows a separate RPG doctrine modifier on **every visible stat row**. Attack and Defense keep the three-part form such as **Attack 16 (18) +2.5M**: 16 is the base creature stat, 18 is the normal non-RPG modified stat, and +2.5M is the persistent doctrine contribution. Damage shows the always-active RPG damage percentage for the unit's current ranged/melee mode; target-health, outnumbering, defensive-target, and random critical conditions are deliberately not folded into this static preview. Morale and Luck show only the doctrine contribution that still fits inside their engine caps. Shots, maximum Hit Points, current Hit Points Left, and Speed explicitly show **+0** because no doctrine directly modifies those stat fields; sustain doctrines still heal through their real combat triggers instead of pretending to increase maximum HP. Every modifier uses the RPG compact formatter, with the same algorithmically generated suffix naming scheme as RPG Renown rather than a fixed K/M/B/T suffix table. The normal good/evil interface treatment is preserved. Each tab contains five upgrades and shows three at a time; use the mouse wheel or scrollbar to reach the others. Every level awards five guild points. Level 2 now requires **75,000 RPG XP**, and each later level requires another **25,000 XP**. This makes early leveling substantially faster and cuts the long-term level-cost ramp in half. RPG levels remain open-ended and saved counters use 64-bit storage. Every 10 kingdom levels now also grants a derived **Guild Prestige Rank**. Prestige does not add hidden combat stats or change the saved guild-point ledger; instead it provides an endgame progression layer: battle Renown gains +2% per Prestige Rank up to +25%, the Steward expands its long-term planning horizon from three ranks up to six, and Elite Rivals gradually become more frequent and able to coordinate more of the player's legitimately purchased doctrine halls. Doctrine progression is **open-ended by default with diminishing returns**. Every safe open-ended primary doctrine keeps improving forever, but each later rank adds less than the rank before it. Percentage/sustain/damage/ward doctrines use logarithmic curves; flat Attack/Defense/Veteran Core now use a gentler logarithmic curve normalized so rank 1 still gives one full stat point. This prevents runaway linear scaling without reintroducing arbitrary hard caps. Only mechanics with a hard engine/semantic safety limit remain capped: Leadership/Fortune at +3, critical/Evasion chance at 100%, Armor/Arcane Piercing at 100% resistance bypass, Close Quarters at 100% melee-penalty recovery, and combined physical/spell damage reduction at 70% to prevent invulnerability. These are **primary-effect caps**, not dead-end progression: Doctrine Ascension can give a capped doctrine a different safe secondary mechanic at ranks 10/25/50/100. No RPG doctrine changes movement or game speed; speed-related mechanics remain excluded from open-ended scaling.

## Tabs and upgrades

The upgrade tree is now built around direct RPG combat mechanics instead of resource payouts or XP multipliers.

All **40 doctrines have been audited against live gameplay call sites**. Every doctrine changes an actual battle/stat mechanic: Attack/Defense, sustain, physical damage/reduction, spell damage/resistance, Morale/Luck, criticals, Evasion, piercing, Close Quarters, or conditional combat states. There are no description-only or UI-only doctrines in the current tree.

**Life steal and Reaper healing never resurrect dead creatures.** They can only repair missing hit points in the currently surviving stack, so their percentages may scale beyond 100% without creating extra creatures. Regeneration follows the same rule and cannot heal beyond the stack's surviving maximum. Critical hits and Evasion use independent combat rolls and remain probability-capped at 100%. Towers do not receive the creature-specific RPG affixes.

### Doctrine Ascension

Every doctrine now has a second progression layer at **ranks 10, 25, 50, and 100**. Reaching those milestones unlocks **Ascension I, II, III, and IV**. Normal doctrine ranks remain open-ended where their primary mechanic is safe, while the four Ascension stages add a separate secondary combat effect rather than merely inflating the doctrine's original number.

Ascension is derived directly from doctrine rank, so it needs **no new save field or profile-version bump**. Respecing a doctrine naturally removes its Ascension stages because its rank returns to zero. The Royal Guild doctrine inspector shows the current Ascension stage, its named evolution, the secondary effect, and the next milestone. **Build Analytics** shows how many doctrines have ascended and the total number of Ascension stages unlocked.

Ascension itself follows diminishing returns too. Percentage-based secondary channels use **100% / 75% / 55% / 40%** of the listed Ascension-I base effect at stages I-IV, for a 2.70x total instead of 4.00x. **Attack and Defense Ascensions are special-cased to guaranteed integer gains of +4 / +3 / +2 / +1**, because Heroes II stores those stats as integers and every Ascension milestone must produce an immediate real game effect. Existing safety ceilings still apply after Ascension: total physical/spell reduction remains capped at 70%, probability mechanics at 100%, and resistance bypass at 100%.

### Doctrine Resonance

Complementary doctrines now create small **Doctrine Resonance** bonuses in the combat math. Offensive doctrine scaling itself is open-ended; resonance bonuses keep their own listed caps, while combined defensive reduction retains its 70% invulnerability-prevention ceiling. Resonance never creates ranks and only works when all participating doctrines are actually trained:

- **Finisher — Executioner + Ruthless:** extra physical damage against targets below half health, capped at +6%.
- **Underdog — Giant Slayer + Bulwark:** extra physical offense while your stack is outnumbered and extra physical reduction while your stack is outnumbered; offensive resonance caps at +5% and defensive resonance at +4%.
- **Last Fury — Frenzy + Last Stand:** extra physical offense below half health and extra physical reduction below half health; offensive resonance caps at +5% and defensive resonance at +4%.
- **Vanguard — Opening Blow + Discipline + Unyielding:** extra opening physical pressure when the attacker and target are still at full starting health, capped at +5%. Discipline + Unyielding also reinforce full-health physical reduction up to +4%.
- **Spell Convergence — Sorcery + matching elemental discipline:** up to +6% additional matching damaging-spell power.
- **Ward Matrix — Spell Ward + matching elemental ward:** up to +6% additional matching spell reduction before Arcane Piercing.

Doctrine inspection now identifies the resonance package associated with a participating doctrine.

Left-click or tap the **BUY** plaque to purchase a rank. Left-click anywhere on an upgrade row (or right-click/hold it) to inspect its exact current effect, next-rank effect, cost, explicit purchase status (READY / NEED / LOCKED / MAX), points already invested, battle triggers recorded, and available guild points. Right-clicking or holding a category tab now lists every doctrine in that hall with its current effect and immediate purchase state instead of showing ranks alone. Battle trigger tracking actively records combat events across physical, spellcraft, morale, luck, and sustain disciplines: Ferocity, Marksman, Brawler, Executioner, Opening Blow, Giant Slayer, Overwhelm, Frenzy, Discipline, Ruthless, Close Quarters, Armor Piercing, Iron Skin, Arrow Ward, Melee Guard, Last Stand, Bulwark, Unyielding, Sorcery, Pyromancy, Cryomancy, Stormcraft, Cataclysm, Arcane Piercing, Spell Ward, elemental wards, Leadership, Fortune, Critical Training, Brutal Criticals, Evasion, Regeneration, Blood Drinker, and Reaper. Doctrines maintain flat purchase costs across all ranks (Arms Training and Armor Training cost 2, Veteran Core costs 3, Leadership/Fortune/proc/sustain cost 2, and standard doctrines cost 1) rather than increasing with each level up. The **Steward** auto-buyer compares approximate expected combat value per point instead of treating +1 Attack and +1% chance as equivalent, skips capped or locked upgrades, gives a modest breadth bonus to new/low-rank doctrines so automated builds discover more mechanics instead of tunneling one efficient line, and recognizes complementary doctrine packages. It learns an emerging **hall-level playstyle** from battle-trigger history plus the points already committed to each hall. The strongest repeatedly-used hall receives a capped preference bonus, while existing investment contributes a smaller commitment bonus, so the Steward deliberately develops the style you are actually playing without permanently locking itself into one line. It now also plans a **Prestige-aware long-term horizon** for every doctrine: the horizon begins at three ranks and increases by one rank per Guild Prestige Rank up to six. The Steward compares average value across that horizon, gives a small durability bonus to doctrines that stay efficient across the whole plan, and applies an 18% commitment preference to the selected goal until that target rank is reached or becomes invalid. Immediate combat value can still override the plan. The Steward's marginal-value model now recognizes **Ascension milestone jumps** and gives capped-primary ranks a small forward-planning value when they are progressing toward a real milestone, so it can deliberately finish an evolution instead of treating rank 4+ Leadership/Fortune as worthless. Steward marginal value is computed from the real next-rank delta, so diminishing primary returns naturally make over-invested doctrines less attractive and encourage broader builds unless a Resonance, Ascension milestone, or playstyle signal justifies specializing further. The Steward now also selects a **package partner** for recognized doctrine combinations: sustain, Marksman/Close Quarters, Executioner/Ruthless, outnumbered Giant Slayer/Bulwark, Frenzy/Last Stand, full-health Opening Blow/Discipline/Unyielding, spellcasting, physical guards, spell wards, Leadership/Fortune, and Critical Training/Brutal Criticals. A useful partner receives a smaller 12% plan preference while the primary long-term goal keeps its 18% preference, so automation can complete a coherent package without abandoning raw value-per-point. The same package logic now has real combat consequences through Doctrine Resonance, so the Steward's complementary purchases can complete mechanical combos instead of being only a scoring preference. The Royal Guild Overview shows **Steward Emerging Focus**, **Steward Long-Term Goal**, and the current **Steward Package Partner**, and its next recommendation uses the same package-aware score as auto-buy. Sustain (Blood Drinker/Reaper/Regeneration), finishing pressure (Executioner/Ruthless), low-health offense/defense (Frenzy/Last Stand), full-health tactics (Opening Blow/Discipline/Unyielding), general/elemental wards, ranged Close Quarters support, spell specialization, and Critical Training/Brutal Criticals can reinforce one another in the Steward's marginal-value score. When two next ranks have exactly the same marginal return it still prefers the lower-ranked doctrine rather than whichever enum entry happens to come first. Press **S** or **A** to toggle Steward.

You can freely reallocate your build at any time using the **Respec** button (or **R** key). The confirmation dialog shows both the refund and the final point balance. Confirming a respec refunds 100% of all guild points spent across every doctrine, resets all ranks to zero, clears historical battle-trigger familiarity so a later Steward session starts from a clean slate, pauses Steward auto-buy, and saves immediately.

Right-click or hold any of the 8 category tabs to inspect that guild hall's doctrinal focus and current purchase state. Doctrine inspection now shows the exact incremental mechanical gain from the next rank as well as its resulting total effect. Click, right-click, or hold the Renown/stats panel (or press **O**) to view the **Royal Guild Overview** modal, which breaks down your total Renown across Hero, Battle, Adventure, and Offline experience, tracks visited adventure sites, shows exact current-level progress and cost, total points invested, active doctrine count, the +5 guild-point reward for the next level, cumulative battle triggers, and your most frequently triggered **Signature Doctrine**. The menu footer reports the visible doctrine row range and all primary keyboard shortcuts, including Space-to-buy and direct 1-8 tab selection. Press **I** to inspect the currently selected doctrine. Press **H** to open the **Royal Guild Help** panel, which summarizes keyboard, mouse/touch controls, the active RPG data folder, autosave behavior, and whether a persistent backup or pending recovery snapshot is available. Press **K** for **Build Analytics**, which combines progression ledger checks, build/combat focus, the Steward's primary/partner roadmap, active Doctrine Resonances, **Ascended Doctrine count / total Ascension stages**, and primary/backup/recovery snapshot status in one diagnostic screen. ## Hero Renown

Victorious adventure heroes now build their own persistent **Hero Renown** alongside kingdom-wide RPG Renown. A hero receives the calculated battle-Renown award whenever that hero wins a battle, and capturing an enemy castle grants an additional **500 Hero Renown**. This personal ledger is deliberately non-combat: it does not change stats, doctrine points, kingdom level, or battle rewards.

Hero Renown is stored in a separate `hero_renown.dat` companion ledger keyed by the engine's stable hero ID, leaving the existing `rpg_profile.dat` format unchanged for save compatibility.

Hero Renown now derives a cosmetic **Hero Legacy** title without adding any new saved fields:

- **Unknown:** 0-999 Renown
- **Proven:** 1,000-4,999 Renown
- **Famous:** 5,000-24,999 Renown
- **Legendary:** 25,000-99,999 Renown
- **Mythic:** 100,000+ Renown

Crossing a threshold displays a concise Hero Legacy notification with the new title. The **Royal Guild Overview** lists the five highest-Renown heroes currently recorded, showing each in-game hero name, current title, total Renown, and progress toward the next title. Mythic heroes are marked as having reached the maximum legacy tier.

### Hero Chronicle

Each hero also has a persistent, cosmetic **Hero Chronicle** that records **battle victories**, **enemy castle captures**, and **Elite Rival victories**. Chronicle deeds never modify combat stats, rewards, doctrine points, kingdom level, AI strength, or Hero Renown.

Chronicle records live in a separate versioned `hero_chronicle.dat` ledger keyed by stable hero ID. The loader rejects unsupported versions, duplicate hero records, negative hero IDs, impossible Elite-win counts greater than total battle wins, excessive record counts, and trailing data. A valid `.bak` ledger is used when the primary is damaged, while writes use a temporary file and backup rotation.

The hero's strongest deed profile derives a deterministic flavour epithet. **Veteran** represents ordinary battle victories, **Castlebreaker** represents castle captures, **Rival-Bane** represents Elite Rival victories, and **Unwritten** represents a hero with no recorded deed. For epithet selection only, castle captures count as four ordinary victories and Elite Rival victories count as twelve, with deterministic tie priority **Rival-Bane > Castlebreaker > Veteran**. These weights are display-only.

The Royal Guild Overview shows the Chronicle epithet and exact deed counts beneath each leading Hero Renown entry.

## XP and economy

RPG XP is now only the progression currency that earns levels and guild points. **Upgrade ranks do not multiply RPG XP and no upgrade pays resource bundles.** Hero XP awards, battles, and first-time adventure actions still feed the persistent RPG profile so ordinary play advances the combat tree.

When the kingdom levels up, the completed level threshold is subtracted from current XP and **all overflow XP carries into the next level**. Large rewards can cross multiple level thresholds in one award; each completed level grants its normal +5 Guild Points, and only the final remainder becomes the new current-level XP. For example, if a level needs 100K XP and the kingdom receives enough to exceed that threshold by 25K, the next level begins at 25K rather than losing the overflow. Lifetime Renown and the Hero/Battle/Adventure/Offline source ledgers still keep the full credited reward for statistics, validation, and long-term history.

The Royal Guild header shows **current-level XP / current level requirement** plus XP remaining, with carried overflow immediately reflected in the new level's bar rather than using lifetime Renown as the active leveling number. The Royal Guild Overview shows both **Current Level XP** and **Lifetime Renown** separately, with algorithmically generated number suffixes. **Guild Prestige** is derived directly from kingdom level at one rank per 10 levels, so it needs no extra save field and cannot be lost independently of the profile. Each Prestige Rank increases battle Renown by +2%, capped at +25%. The Royal Guild Overview shows current Prestige Rank, the next level milestone, the active battle-Renown bonus, Steward planning horizon, Elite Rival chance, and Elite Rival focus-hall limit. Battle activity adds a modest RPG XP bonus that scales with battle experience, outcome, and the opposing profile's level. Victorious castle sieges grant an additional +25% bonus experience, winning defensive stands grant +10%, and defeating a stronger RPG profile earns an additional heroic-underdog bonus without rewarding deliberate losses. Siege and defense bonuses stack when a castle defender wins a siege. Normal hero experience still contributes RPG XP as well, so the battle-specific coefficient is intentionally smaller to avoid excessive double progression. Adventure XP scales with RPG level and is awarded once per map tile for each profile, preventing repeated farming of the same site. Level 1 uses exactly the configured base reward and logarithmic level scaling begins only after the first RPG level. Trivial lore interactions such as Signs/Bottles now award little Renown, character-growth sites pay more, and dangerous/puzzle destinations such as Daemon Caves, Graveyards, Shipwrecks, Sphinxes, and Pyramids are deliberately more rewarding.

Opposing kingdoms receive deterministic temporary RPG profiles derived only from combat ranks the local profile has actually purchased. Ordinary enemy sophistication still scales with the 85–115% kingdom strength roll: weaker rolls coordinate one invested hall, roughly equal opponents coordinate two, and stronger rolls at 105% or above can coordinate up to three. From RPG level 5 onward, each hostile kingdom also has a deterministic chance to become an **Elite Rival** on that map.

### Elite Rival Mutations

Every Elite Rival now receives deterministic **combat mutations** for the current map. These are separate encounter affixes—not doctrine ranks—so they can make an Elite mechanically distinctive without violating the rule that temporary rival doctrine ranks only come from doctrines the player has actually purchased. Elites start with **1 mutation**; Guild Prestige adds a second at Prestige 3 and a third at Prestige 6, with no further stacking beyond three.

- **Vampiric:** +8% life steal.
- **Berserker:** +10% physical damage.
- **Armored:** +8% physical reduction.
- **Arcane-Shielded:** +8% spell reduction.
- **Regenerating:** +5% start-of-turn Regeneration.
- **Deadly:** +4% critical chance and +10% critical bonus damage.
- **Elusive:** +6% Evasion chance.
- **Piercing:** +10% Armor Piercing and +10% Arcane Piercing.
- **Unstable Magic:** +12% damaging-spell power.
- **Warforged:** +3 creature Attack and +3 creature Defense.

Mutation effects use the same live combat accessors as doctrines and Ascensions, so they affect actual attacks, damage reduction, spell damage, spell mitigation, crit rolls, Evasion rolls, healing, and unit stats. Existing 70% mitigation, 100% probability, and 100% piercing safety ceilings still apply. Defeating an Elite gains an additional **+5% battle Renown per active mutation** on top of the existing Elite/Prestige reward bonuses. **Elite Rival Intel (V)** lists each active Elite color and its exact mutations plus a full effect legend before you engage them.

The Elite encounter chance still starts low, but Guild Prestige now contributes to the late-game rise and the final chance caps at 40% per hostile kingdom. Elite rivals roll only in the 100–115% band and still receive stronger weighting for legitimate purchased doctrine packages. Their hall-selection priority now blends the player's points invested with actual battle-trigger history, so an Elite Rival increasingly recognizes the doctrine halls the player truly uses instead of looking only at the static build sheet. Each Elite Rival also receives a deterministic tactical archetype for the map: **Warlord** favors Army/Command, **Predator** favors Offense/Tactics, **Arcanist** favors Magic/Wards, **Sentinel** favors Defense/Wards, and **Trickster** favors Tactics/Mastery. Archetypes now influence both hall selection and **specific doctrine weighting** inside those halls—for example Predators prefer finishing/outnumbering pressure, Arcanists prefer Sorcery/elemental/piercing ranks, and Sentinels prefer physical and magical guards. These are bounded percentage biases inside the existing generation algorithm; every generated rank is still clamped to the normal 115% maximum and an enemy still cannot receive any doctrine the player has not purchased. Elite rivals begin with up to four coordinated invested halls and can reach five and then six halls at higher Prestige. Every rank remains clamped to the same maximum 115% envelope and no enemy receives a doctrine the player has not purchased. Victories against elite rivals begin at the existing **+35% RPG Renown** premium and gain +1 percentage point per Prestige Rank up to a +50% elite premium, in addition to the general Prestige battle-Renown bonus. Neutral monsters remain deliberately simpler, using one focused hall inside their weaker 60–90% range. The Royal Guild Overview shows how many elite rival kingdoms exist on the current map and breaks them down by archetype. Press **V** in the Royal Guild for a dedicated Elite Rival Intel panel with archetype descriptions and the current encounter/focus limits. Temporary profiles and elite status are discarded when the map ends.

Offline progress still grants RPG XP for the full elapsed interval with no duration cap. It does not grant normal game resources or recruit creatures. The game's normal economy, armies, and map rules remain in control of those systems. A guaranteed baseline daily equivalent ensures that even Day-1 profiles and new maps advance persistently while offline. When returning to the game, the offline progress popup is deliberately compact: it shows only how long you were away plus RPG XP and virtual offline rewards as native experience/resource sprites. Streaks, contracts, maps, discoveries, meters, promotions, readiness details, and treasury explanations no longer clutter the popup. The active-map treasury remains unchanged; the resource sprites represent the same virtual offline values used to calculate persistent RPG XP. A qualifying homecoming can advance the streak at most once per **local calendar day**; returning again on the same local date does not farm extra streak days, and missing a local calendar day resets the streak. Day boundaries follow the system's local time instead of UTC.

## AI integration

Adventure and combat AI are fully aware of RPG doctrines and stats:
- **Adventure AI:** Strategic army strength uses RPG Attack, Defense, conditional damage/reduction, expected critical damage, Evasion, sustain, Morale, and Luck. Negative Luck uses its real half-damage expected loss instead of being valued like a full missed attack. For the active persistent kingdom, target scoring now includes the unclaimed RPG Renown available at each site: Scouts care most, Hunters strongly value discoveries, Fighters/Champions receive a smaller progression incentive, and Couriers only take modest RPG detours. Morale/Luck secondary skills and adventure buff sites respect the +3 caps after kingdom RPG bonuses are applied, so AI heroes stop detouring for capped benefits. Revealed Witch Huts offering already-capped Leadership/Luck are skipped. Wisdom and Mysticism value the strongest relevant elemental specialization rather than pretending every element applies at once, Archery recognizes Close Quarters synergy, damage-spell shrines use the active RPG multiplier, and Magic Wells/Artesian Springs become more attractive to developed magic kingdoms.

## Doctrine live-effect audit

A fresh post-Ascension audit verifies **40 / 40 doctrines** and **40 / 40 doctrine Ascensions** have direct gameplay effects. The 40 Ascensions map to 13 secondary combat channels, and every one of those channels is consumed by live battle/stat code. The current 40-doctrine tree maps to these live gameplay channels:

- **ARMY:** Arms Training, Armor Training, and Veteran Core feed creature Attack/Defense; Blood Drinker, Reaper, and Regeneration heal surviving stacks through real combat events.
- **OFFENSE / TACTICS / DEFENSE / MASTERY physical doctrines:** feed the shared physical damage/reduction calculation using ranged/melee, health-state, and outnumbering conditions; Armor Piercing changes actual RPG mitigation.
- **MAGIC / WARDS:** Sorcery and elemental disciplines change damaging spell output; Pyromancy/Cryomancy/Stormcraft/Cataclysm are selected through the live spell-specialization mapping before `spellMultiplier()` applies them. Spell Ward and elemental wards change incoming damaging spell reduction through the matching ward mapping; Arcane Piercing bypasses that mitigation.
- **COMMAND / remaining MASTERY:** Leadership and Fortune alter combat Morale/Luck, Critical Training and Brutal Criticals alter real attack rolls/damage, Evasion rolls against incoming creature attacks, and Close Quarters changes the actual ranged-melee penalty.

This audit is intentionally about **battle-side effects**, not just AI valuation or tooltips. Ascension Attack/Defense channels use guaranteed +4/+3/+2/+1 integer stage gains so no milestone can disappear into fractional stat rounding; percentage-based Ascension stages remain direct floating-point combat modifiers.

Diminishing-return scaling is also battle-side rather than cosmetic: the same reduced primary values feed creature Attack/Defense, physical/spell multipliers, sustain, mitigation, criticals, Evasion, and piercing. The Royal Guild next-rank preview therefore reports the actual smaller marginal gain rather than displaying a fixed per-rank promise.

The unit-info modifier layer mirrors those mechanics without inventing fake stat bonuses: Attack/Defense display their exact persistent 64-bit doctrine contribution, Damage displays baseline physical doctrine scaling (including Close Quarters recovery when a shooter is currently engaged), Morale/Luck display their effective capped contribution, and non-modified fields display +0 so speed/shot/HP exclusions are visible rather than implicit.

## Saves

Profile format version 6 performs a one-time respec when loading older RPG profiles: old slot ranks are cleared, the free guild-point balance is reconstructed directly from the saved kingdom level, battle-trigger familiarity is reset, and Steward auto-buy is disabled so the restored points are not immediately spent for you. Profile format version 7 introduces the easier RPG level curve. Profile format version 8 introduces 2x faster leveling (50,000 base + 25,000 per level) and flat doctrine costs across all ranks. Existing version-7 profiles are validated against the version-7 curve and cost ledger; doctrine ranks are preserved, any excess points previously spent under escalating costs are refunded to available guild points, and progress crossing the new cheaper thresholds awards levels immediately. The migrated profile is saved immediately.

The **Royal Guild Overview** now includes a **PROFILE & RECOVERY** section showing the active data folder, whether the persistent RPG profile backup is ready, whether a complete temporary recovery snapshot is waiting, and which RPG actions autosave. **Build Analytics** adds a live validation of the current profile's closed guild-point/Renown ledger and reports whether the primary, backup, and temporary recovery snapshots currently exist. The same information is available from **H** in Royal Guild Help, so profile safety is visible in-game instead of only being documented externally.

On Windows, RPG data, offline state, and standard game saves live in **Documents\\Homm2RPG**. `rpg_profile.dat` contains the RPG profile and the adventure tiles already rewarded. `offline_progress.dat` contains the offline timestamp and state. Normal game save files are in the same folder. The game copies older RPG and save files into this folder on first use without overwriting files already there. Both RPG profile and offline progress writes use temporary files and preserve persistent `.bak` backups (`rpg_profile.dat.bak` and `offline_progress.dat.bak`) during replacement for crash resilience. RPG profile writes also revalidate the complete runtime ledger before touching the current file, so an impossible in-memory state cannot overwrite the last recoverable profile. RPG profile recovery also rejects current-format snapshots whose doctrine ranks exceed a remaining hard safety cap, whose Brutal Criticals rank violates its Critical Training prerequisite, whose guild-point ledger cannot have been produced by normal progression, whose total/field/offline Renown source ledgers disagree, whose stored current-level XP is already large enough to require another level because normal runtime processing should already have consumed all affordable thresholds, whose visited-site list contains impossible duplicates, or whose record has unexpected trailing tokens, so a parseable but damaged file cannot displace a healthy recovery copy. RPG profile and offline-progress recovery preserve known-good backups when a primary file is corrupt, and fully written temporary snapshots are retained if promotion fails so they remain available on the next launch. RPG profile recovery also prefers a complete temporary snapshot when filesystem timestamps tie. Offline format version 13 preserves the version-12 durable pending-resume state and also stores the earned Homecoming Streak's local calendar-day index. Older offline formats remain readable and migrate the existing streak timestamp to a local day once, preventing later daylight-saving or timezone changes from reinterpreting the day that earned the streak. Crash recovery prefers a valid temporary snapshot when its logical timestamp ties the primary because it represents the newest completed write candidate. The kingdom profile persists across maps and loaded saves.

The RPG profile uses 64-bit counters. Most doctrines scale indefinitely. Hard-capped primary mechanics may still advance through rank 100 when those ranks unlock Doctrine Ascension stages; after the final Ascension milestone, a capped doctrine stops accepting ranks that would have no remaining mechanical effect. The suffix formatter itself has no fixed suffix list. The game's original hero levels and resources retain their own engine limits. Older offline snapshots with creature-roster fields remain readable, but those fields are ignored and are omitted from new snapshots.
